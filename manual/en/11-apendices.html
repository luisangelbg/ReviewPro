AThe files the app reads and writes

ReviewPro runs in the browser and sends nothing to any server: every file comes in from and goes out to your computer. This appendix gathers them.

A.1 · What the app reads

BThe decision rules, gathered together

The scales and thresholds the app uses or suggests, in a single list. They are guides for deciding, not laws: what you know about the topic and your studies always comes first.

BlockQuantityScale
1WSS@95 of prioritized screeningWork saved over random order to reach 95% of the relevant records; higher is better.
4Recall at stopping≥ 95% good · 90–95% acceptable with verification · < 90% too many were missed
4Cohen’s kappa (Landis and Koch 1977)≤ 0 poor · 0.01–0.20 slight · 0.21–0.40 fair · 0.41–0.60 moderate · 0.61–0.80 substantial · 0.81–1 almost perfect
4Pilot kappaBelow the protocol’s target (0.6 by default): refine the criteria before going on.
7Overall risk-of-bias judgmentThe worst of the domains; also “high” with three or more domains with some concerns.
7GRADE · risk of bias−2 if > 75% of the weight is at high risk · −1 if ≥ 50%, or high + some concerns > 80%
7GRADE · inconsistency−1 if I² > 75%, or > 50% with the prediction interval crossing the null
7GRADE · imprecision−1 if the CI crosses the null or there are < 400 experimental units · −2 if both
7GRADE · publication bias−1 if Egger p < 0.10 with ≥ 10 studies; with fewer, it is not assessed
8I² (Higgins and Thompson 2002)≈ 25% low · ≈ 50% moderate · ≈ 75% high (a convention; also read τ² and the prediction interval)
8Prediction intervalAway from the null: consistent effect · crosses the null: works on average, not everywhere
8Asymmetry testsWith < 10 studies or similar standard errors, only as a reference
8Sensitivityholds · changes significance · changes sign
10Export resolution150 dpi screen · 300 print and most journals · 600 demanding journals · 900 posters

CGlossary

TermWhat it is
Certainty of the evidenceHow confident one can be that the true effect is close to the estimate; with GRADE, high, moderate, low or very low.
DuplicateThe same record obtained from two or more sources.
Effect sizeThe number that summarizes the result of a comparison on a common scale.
GRADEThe system for rating the certainty of the evidence per outcome.
Gray literatureTheses, technical reports, conference abstracts and other documents outside peer-reviewed journals.
HeterogeneityThe variation between the true effects of the studies; it is measured with τ², I² and the prediction interval.
I²The fraction of the observed variation that is not due to sampling chance.
KappaThe agreement between reviewers corrected for the agreement expected by chance.
Live datumA {{…}} mark in Block 9 that is replaced by a number or a table from the project when displayed or exported.
lnRRThe log response ratio: ln(treated mean / control mean).
Meta-regressionThe regression of the effects on characteristics of the studies (moderators).
ModeratorA characteristic of the studies that may explain the heterogeneity.
Multilevel modelThe random-effects model with one level between studies and another between effects of the same study.
PICO, PECO, PCC, SPIDERFrameworks for formulating the question of a review.
Pooled effectThe weighted average of the effects of the studies, with its confidence interval.
Prediction intervalThe range where the true effect of a new study or site would fall.
PRISMA 2020, PRISMA-ScR, PRISMA-PThe reporting guidelines for systematic reviews, scoping reviews and protocols.
ProtocolThe plan of the review, written and registered before searching.
Publication biasThe preferential publication of studies with favorable or significant results.
Random effectsThe model that assumes each study has its own true effect, drawn from a distribution.
Risk of biasThe possibility that the design or conduct of a study pulls its result away from the truth.
SANRAThe quality scale for narrative reviews.
ScreeningThe selection of records by title and abstract, before reading the full text.
Search stringThe combination of terms with Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) that is run in a database.
Stopping ruleThe criterion, declared in advance, for stopping screening before reading every record.
Study · report · recordA piece of research; a document that reports it; a bibliographic entry. A study may have several reports and a report several records.
Summary of findingsThe table with the effect and the certainty of each outcome.
SWiMThe guideline for reporting synthesis without meta-analysis.
τ²The variance of the true effects between studies.
Trim-and-fillThe method that estimates how many studies would be missing for the funnel to be symmetric.

DWhen something goes wrong

What happensWhat to do
When I open the app, my project is not thereThe project lives in the browser where you worked on it. Open it with Open file… from your latest .reviewpro.json. Save the file at the end of every session.
A database file does not import, or fewer records come inCheck that the export includes all the results and the complete fields; if it is a table, that it has headers. Compare with the Imported column of the search log.
A duplicate slipped throughExclude it in screening with the duplicate-publication criterion.
The “open” link of the PDF does not workOpen the app by double-clicking index.html, not from the server; or copy the path.
Another reviewer’s decisions do not match my recordsBoth of you must have imported the same files: share the project and have the other reviewer open it before screening.
Block 8 says there are not enough dataAt least two studies with a complete effect in the outcome are needed; check the incomplete rows in Block 6.
The response ratio is not computedIt needs positive means and the n of both groups. If the variable can be zero or negative, use the standardized mean difference.
The checker flags a sentence that is fineRead it again; if it is your own generalization and not a result, leave it. The checker is strict on purpose.
A live datum appears in redIts block does not compute it yet, or the outcome name does not match: choose it from the Live datum menu.
A figure does not appear in Block 10Its block has not drawn it: visit it (or use ↻ Refresh from the blocks).
I want to check that the app computes correctlyWith the local server (Open ReviewPro.bat), open http://localhost:9550/tests/: more than 550 checks against published results and independent implementations.

FThe tool: license and how to cite it

F.1 · What is inside the app

ReviewPro is written in HTML, CSS and JavaScript without any third-party library: not for the charts, not for reading the bibliographic formats, not for the screening model, not for the meta-analysis, not for writing the Word document or the .zip. The methods are those of the works in Appendix E, and the computation engines were verified against published results—the BCG dataset (Colditz et al. 1994), the examples of Borenstein et al. (2009) and of Fleiss (1971)—and against independent implementations. The example project, the test files and the labs are fictitious.
